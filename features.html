Six systems. Zero wasted watts.

Each subsystem tackles a specific way that Mars drains a rover's power. The animations below are simplified simulations of how each one behaves.

01 Generate

Maximum Power Point Tracking

A solar panel only gives its full power at one point on its voltage–current curve, and that point moves whenever the light changes. Our MPPT system finds that point and stays there.

  • Buck/boost DC-DC converter between the array and the rest of the system
  • Perturb & Observe or Incremental Conductance algorithms
  • Keeps adjusting voltage and current to sit at the peak of the curve
  • Works whichever way the panels face
02 Generate

Dust Mitigation

Past rovers waited for lucky wind gusts to blow their panels clean. ARES cleans its own panels: when dust builds up, the panel is shaken until the dust slides off.

  • Primary method: piezoelectric vibration. It's simple and cheap.
  • Uses quartz crystal's natural piezo effect, or sound/buzzer vibration
  • Shakes dust loose and lets sand trickle off the panel surface
  • Triggers automatically when output drops because of dust
03 Manage

Tiered Survival Mode

When power and solar gain drop, ARES doesn't just shut down. It steps down through set tiers, cutting non-essential systems first and keeping the core signal alive to the end.

  • Nominal → Reduced Operations → Minimal Operations → Deep Hibernation
  • Each tier has a fixed list of which subsystems stay powered
  • Communications are the last thing to go, so the rover can always be found
  • Steps back up automatically when the sun returns
04 Distribute

Dynamic Load Prioritization

Not every subsystem matters equally at every moment. ARES gives power out by priority and need, so the critical systems are served first when there isn't enough to go around.

  • Each subsystem has a priority level and a power requirement
  • Power goes out from highest to lowest priority
  • Low-priority loads are reduced or shed when supply drops
  • Recalculated continuously as available power changes
05 Generate

Tilt Assist

A rover parked on a slope points its panels at the ground instead of the sky. Tilt assist levels each panel on its own and tilts it toward the best sun angle, wherever the rover stands.

  • Levels each panel independently of the rover body
  • Works with the rover's mobility to find the best sun angle
  • Tracks the sun across the sky through the sol
  • Directly addresses the failure that ended Spirit
06 Manage

Mission Control Telemetry

Everything ARES does is visible as it happens. The dashboard shows how much power is coming in, where it's going and how much is being lost to dust or fading light.

  • Live I-V and P-V characteristic curves
  • Real-time power efficiency and battery state of charge
  • Active power rails, updating at 60 Hz
  • Calculates in real time how much power is lost when light fades or dust collects
