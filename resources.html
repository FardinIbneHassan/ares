03 / FAQ

Frequently asked questions

What is ARES?

ARES (Autonomous Rover Energy System) is an energy module for Mars rovers. It handles generating, distributing and managing power, so the rover gets the most from its solar panels and survives when energy is low.

Is ARES actually going to Mars?

No. ARES is a student-built prototype. It is designed around problems real Mars rovers faced, and our goal is to use it in national and international rover competitions.

Who is building it?

Team Solaris Tech, four students who want to start Dhaka University's first rover team. Member profiles are coming soon on the Team page.

What is MPPT and why does it matter?

Maximum Power Point Tracking. A solar panel delivers its most power at one specific voltage, and that voltage shifts as the light changes. An MPPT converter keeps finding that point, so less energy is wasted.

How do you clean dust off the panels?

With vibration. Piezoelectric elements (or a buzzer) shake the panel so dust loosens and slides off. It's simple, cheap and has no moving brushes to wear out.

What happens when the power runs low?

ARES enters tiered survival mode. It steps from Nominal to Reduced Operations, then Minimal Operations, then Deep Hibernation, switching off non-essential systems at each step while keeping the communication signal alive.

What stage is the project at?

Prototyping. We're combining software simulation, hardware and 3D design/printing. Results will be published on this page as soon as testing begins.

How can I support or join?

Head to the Contact page. We welcome sponsors, mentors and anyone interested in building rovers with us.
